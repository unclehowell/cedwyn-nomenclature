River Ely (Afon Llai)

Ely is the name of the river that runs past Leckwith and Llandough to the sea at Penarth. A work printed in Cardiff in 1840 by Captain W. H. Smyth, R.N., glosses the name in a footnote: “Ely is a dimunitive, or corruption of Afon Llai, the mud river.”[1] A 1997 book on Penarth place-names repeats this gloss.[2]

Place-name article · first edition 7 November 2026

The 1840 source

The title page reads: Nautical Observations on the Port and Maritime Vicinity of Cardiff, with occasional strictures on the Ninth Report of the Taff Vale Railway Directors; and some general remarks on the commerce of Glamorganshire, by Captain W. H. Smyth, R.N., K.S.F., Cardiff, printed by W. Bird, Duke-Street, 1840. Its introduction dates the writing to the end of August 1839, after the Taff Vale Railway shareholders’ meeting.[1]

On page 13 the text discusses whether the Ely or Cogan Pil was the better place for a harbour, and a footnote reads: “Pil, in Welsh, is a small inlet filled by the tide. Ely is a dimunitive, or corruption of Afon Llai, the mud river.” (spelling as printed). The same pages use “the Ely” as the river’s name.[1]

What the source shows, and what stays open

  • Shown: in 1840 Smyth printed the derivation “Ely … corruption of Afon Llai, the mud river”. This is the author’s explanation of the name, printed in a work about the port.[1]
  • Not shown: the footnote is not an earlier dated use of the form Afon Llai in a record. Whether Welsh place-name scholarship accepts the derivation is not checked here.
  • Secondary account: Thorne (1997) describes Smyth as Marine Superintendent to Lord Bute and the passage as evidence given to Parliament in 1840. The 1840 item located is a work printed in Cardiff; a parliamentary version of the same words is not checked.[2]
